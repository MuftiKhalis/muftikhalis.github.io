Reading vegetation from space, one 10-metre pixel at a time.

I’m Mufti Khalis, a biology graduate from Universitas Syiah Kuala in Banda Aceh. This is my small corner of the web: a few handy tools, a plain-language look at remote sensing, and English study links.

NDVI, explained simply

Healthy plants absorb red light for photosynthesis and reflect a lot of near-infrared light (NIR). NDVI compares the two, so a satellite can tell green, growing vegetation from bare ground or water.

The result runs from −1 to 1. Roughly: water is below 0, bare ground and buildings sit close to 0, and dense, healthy vegetation is high, often above 0.5. Exact cut-offs depend on the sensor and the place.

NDVI = (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red)

  • Waterbelow 0
  • Bare ground, built-up0 to 0.1
  • Sparse vegetation0.1 to 0.3
  • Moderate vegetation0.3 to 0.5
  • Dense vegetationabove 0.5
